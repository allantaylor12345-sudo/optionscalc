How to use the FIRE calculator

  1. Enter what you expect to spend per year once you stop working.
  2. Enter your current investments and how much you save each month.
  3. Use a real return (after inflation), such as 5% to 7% for a stock-heavy portfolio.
  4. Pick a withdrawal rate. 4% is the classic rule of thumb; 3% to 3.5% is more conservative for early retirement.

Your FIRE number

FIRE number = annual expenses ÷ withdrawal rate. With $40,000 of expenses and a 4% withdrawal rate, you need $1,000,000. At that point your portfolio can, historically, fund your spending for decades.

Coast FIRE

Coast FIRE is the amount you need invested today so that, without saving another dollar, growth alone takes you to your FIRE number by your target retirement age. Once you reach it, you only need to earn enough to cover your current expenses.

Living off dividends

Some investors prefer to live only on dividends instead of selling shares. In that case, use your portfolio's dividend yield as the withdrawal rate. The dividend reinvestment calculator shows how that income grows over time, and selling covered calls can add more income on top.

Assumptions

The calculator uses a constant return and constant savings. Real markets go up and down, and the order of returns matters, especially right after you retire. Treat the result as a planning estimate.

Frequently asked questions

What does FIRE stand for?

Financial Independence, Retire Early: saving and investing enough that your investments can cover your living expenses.

Is the 4% rule safe?

It comes from historical U.S. data over 30-year retirements. For longer retirements, many people use 3% to 3.5% to add a margin of safety.

Should I use nominal or real returns?

Use real (after-inflation) returns, because your expenses are in today's dollars.

What is Coast FIRE?

The amount you need invested now so that growth alone reaches your FIRE number by your target age, with no more contributions.
