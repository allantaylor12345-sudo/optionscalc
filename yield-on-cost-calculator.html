How to use it

  1. Add each purchase: number of shares and the price you paid.
  2. Enter the annual dividend per share (the last quarterly dividend × 4 is a good estimate) and the current share price.
  3. Read your average cost, yield on cost, current yield and income.

Average cost per share

Average cost = total amount invested ÷ total shares. If you bought 50 shares at $40 and 30 at $35, your average cost is ($2,000 + $1,050) ÷ 80 = $38.13.

Yield on cost vs current yield

  • Yield on cost = annual dividend per share ÷ your average cost. It shows the return on the money you put in.
  • Current yield = annual dividend per share ÷ today's price. It shows what a new buyer gets.

When a company keeps raising its dividend, your yield on cost climbs year after year even though the current yield may stay flat. That's why long-term dividend investors track it.

A note on yield on cost

Yield on cost is useful for tracking income growth, but it should not decide whether to keep or sell a stock. To compare investments, use the current yield and the current value of your shares.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good yield on cost?

It depends on how long you've held the stock. After 10 or more years with a growing dividend, many investors see yields on cost of 6% to 10% or more.

Does yield on cost include reinvested dividends?

If you add reinvested dividends as purchases in the table, they are included in your average cost, which lowers your yield on cost slightly but increases your income.

How do I find the annual dividend per share?

Use the most recent dividend multiplied by the number of payments per year, for example a $0.45 quarterly dividend is $1.80 per year.

Is average cost the same as cost basis?

For one stock, yes, before adjustments such as return of capital or stock splits.
