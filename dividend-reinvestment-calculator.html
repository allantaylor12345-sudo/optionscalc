How to use the DRIP calculator

  1. Enter what you invest today and what you add every month.
  2. Enter the stock's (or fund's) dividend yield and how fast you expect the dividend and the share price to grow.
  3. Set your tax on dividends (0% for tax-advantaged accounts) and how often dividends are paid.
  4. Turn reinvestment on and off to see the difference DRIP makes.

What dividend reinvestment does

With a dividend reinvestment plan (DRIP), every dividend buys more shares. Those new shares pay dividends too, so your share count grows even without new money. Over long periods, this compounding, plus companies raising their dividends, is what turns a modest yield into a large income.

Yield on cost

Yield on cost is your annual dividend income divided by the money you actually invested. A stock bought with a 3% yield that grows its dividend 7% a year will pay close to 6% on your original cost after 10 years.

Assumptions

The calculator assumes constant yield, dividend growth and price growth, and buys fractional shares. Real dividends can be cut, and prices fluctuate. Use conservative numbers and treat the result as an estimate, not a forecast.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to reinvest dividends?

If you don't need the income now, reinvesting usually leads to a larger portfolio and larger future income because of compounding. Turn DRIP off in the calculator to compare.

What dividend growth rate should I use?

Many established dividend growers have raised dividends 4% to 8% a year historically. Use a lower number to be conservative.

Are reinvested dividends taxed?

In a regular taxable account, yes: dividends are taxed in the year they are paid even if reinvested. Set the tax field to 0% for tax-advantaged accounts.

How much do I need to live off dividends?

Divide your yearly expenses by your portfolio's dividend yield. With $40,000 of expenses and a 4% yield, you'd need about $1,000,000. See the FIRE calculator for more.
